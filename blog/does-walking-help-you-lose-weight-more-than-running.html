Does Walking Help You Lose Weight More Than Running?

Aug 05, 2026 7 min read

The question of whether walking or running is superior for weight loss is a common one, and the answer isn't as simple as picking one over the other. While running generally burns more calories in a shorter amount of time due to its higher intensity, walking workouts can be an incredibly powerful and sustainable tool for weight loss, especially when done consistently, for longer durations, or at a brisk pace. The most effective activity is ultimately the one you can stick with long-term, integrating it into a holistic approach that includes nutrition and lifestyle changes.

How Do Walking and Running Contribute to Weight Loss?

Both walking and running are excellent forms of cardiovascular exercise that contribute to weight loss primarily by increasing your caloric expenditure. Weight loss occurs when you consistently create a calorie deficit, meaning you burn more calories than you consume. While diet plays a crucial role, exercise helps widen this deficit.

  • Calorie Burn: The most direct way exercise aids weight loss is through burning calories. Higher intensity generally means more calories burned per unit of time.
  • Metabolic Boost: Regular physical activity can slightly boost your metabolism, helping your body burn more calories even at rest.
  • Muscle Preservation: Unlike restrictive dieting alone, incorporating exercise, especially strength training alongside cardio, helps preserve lean muscle mass, which is metabolically active tissue. To support this muscle preservation during weight loss, especially when engaging in activities like walking or running, ensuring adequate dietary protein intake is essential.
  • Improved Mood and Energy: Exercise can reduce stress and improve sleep, both of which are factors that can indirectly support weight loss efforts by reducing stress-eating or improving energy for healthy choices.

How Many Calories Does Walking Burn Compared to Running?

When comparing calorie burn, running typically comes out ahead per unit of time. For example, a person weighing 150 pounds might burn approximately:

  • Running: 400-600 calories per hour (at a moderate pace of 6 mph).
  • Walking: 200-350 calories per hour (at a brisk pace of 3.5 mph).

However, this doesn't tell the whole story. The total calories burned depend on several factors:

  • Body Weight: Heavier individuals burn more calories for the same activity because their bodies expend more energy to move.
  • Intensity: A faster pace, uphill incline, or incorporating intervals (e.g., power walking bursts) will increase calorie expenditure for walking. Similarly, a faster running pace or inclines will burn more calories.
  • Duration: While running burns more per minute, if you walk for a longer duration, you can potentially match or even exceed the total calorie burn of a shorter run. For instance, walking for 90 minutes might burn a similar amount of calories as running for 45 minutes.
  • Individual Metabolism: Everyone's metabolism is slightly different, influencing calorie burn.

What truly matters for weight loss is the total cumulative calorie deficit over time. If you can walk for significantly longer and more consistently than you can run, walking could lead to more substantial weight loss.

What Are the Advantages of Walking for Weight Loss?

Walking offers numerous benefits that make it an excellent choice for weight loss, particularly for beginners or those seeking a sustainable approach:

  • Lower Impact and Reduced Injury Risk: Walking is much gentler on your joints compared to running, which involves significant impact with each stride. This makes it ideal for individuals with joint pain, those new to exercise, or those carrying excess weight who may be more prone to injuries from high-impact activities.
  • Accessibility and Convenience: You don't need special equipment beyond comfortable shoes, and you can walk almost anywhere, anytime. This removes many barriers to starting and maintaining an exercise routine.
  • Sustainability and Consistency: Because it's lower impact and less demanding, many people find it easier to walk consistently day after day. Consistency is the cornerstone of successful weight loss.
  • Mental Health Benefits: Walking can be a meditative and stress-reducing activity, helping to manage emotional eating and improve overall well-being.
  • Versatility: You can easily increase the intensity of your walks by adding hills, carrying light weights, using a treadmill incline, or incorporating brisk intervals (power walking).

What Are the Advantages of Running for Weight Loss?

Running also brings a host of benefits that make it a powerful tool for weight loss:

  • Higher Calorie Burn in Less Time: For those with limited time, running allows you to burn more calories in a shorter period, making it a time-efficient workout.
  • Improved Cardiovascular Fitness: Running significantly boosts cardiovascular health, strengthening your heart and lungs more rapidly than walking.
  • "Afterburn Effect" (EPOC): Higher intensity exercises like running can lead to a greater EPOC (Excess Post-exercise Oxygen Consumption), also known as the "afterburn effect." This means your body continues to burn calories at an elevated rate for some time after your workout ends as it recovers.
  • Bone Density: The weight-bearing impact of running can help improve bone density, which is important for long-term skeletal health.

Which is Better for Sustained Weight Loss: Walking or Running?

The truth is, neither walking nor running is inherently "better" for sustained weight loss; rather, the most effective activity is the one you can perform consistently over the long term and that aligns with your individual circumstances. Here's how to decide:

Consider Your Fitness Level and Health

  • Beginners or those with joint issues: Walking is often the safer, more accessible starting point. Gradually increase duration and intensity.
  • Experienced exercisers: Running can offer a more intense challenge and higher calorie burn in less time.

Prioritize Consistency

A moderate activity you do six days a week will yield better results than an intense activity you only manage once a week. If running feels daunting or leads to burnout, walking more frequently might be your superior choice.

Think About Your Goals

  • Time-efficient calorie burn: Running might be preferable.
  • Building a sustainable habit with lower injury risk: Walking is excellent.
  • Overall fitness and health: Both contribute significantly, and combining them (e.g., walking on recovery days, running on others) can be highly effective.

Don't Forget Nutrition

It's crucial to remember that exercise, whether walking or running, is only one part of the weight loss equation. Nutrition plays a paramount role. You cannot out-exercise a consistently poor diet. Focusing on a balanced diet rich in whole foods, lean proteins, healthy fats, and complex carbohydrates, while managing portion sizes, is essential for creating the necessary calorie deficit.

Key Takeaways for Choosing Between Walking and Running

Ultimately, both walking and running are fantastic forms of exercise for weight loss. The key is to find what works best for YOU:

  1. Prioritize Consistency: The activity you can do regularly is the most effective.
  2. Consider Intensity: For walking, incorporate inclines, brisk intervals, or longer durations to boost calorie burn. For running, vary your pace and distance.
  3. Listen to Your Body: Avoid injury. If running causes pain, opt for walking or a run/walk strategy.
  4. Combine with Nutrition: Exercise amplifies your results, but a healthy, balanced diet creates the primary calorie deficit needed for weight loss.
  5. Make it Enjoyable: If you enjoy your exercise, you're far more likely to stick with it. Find routes you love, listen to music or podcasts, or exercise with a friend.

No matter your choice, moving your body is a powerful step towards a healthier, lighter you. Start today, and let consistency be your guide.
