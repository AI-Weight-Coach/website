How Long Does It Take to Lose a Stone?

Apr 04, 2026 5 min read

Losing a stone (which is equal to 14 pounds or approximately 6.35 kilograms) is a common weight loss goal, and the time it takes can vary significantly from person to person. Generally, aiming for a healthy and sustainable weight loss rate of 1 to 2 pounds per week, you can expect to lose a stone in approximately 7 to 14 weeks.

This timeframe is an average, and several factors will influence your personal journey. Understanding these elements can help you set realistic expectations and create a more effective weight loss plan. What Factors Influence the Rate of Weight Loss?

Weight loss is a complex process, and while the basic principle involves creating a calorie deficit, the speed at which you lose weight is affected by numerous individual characteristics and lifestyle choices.

Your Starting Weight and Body Composition

  • Higher Starting Weight: Individuals with a higher starting weight often see more rapid initial weight loss. This is because a larger body typically burns more calories at rest, making it easier to create a significant calorie deficit.
  • Body Composition: People with more muscle mass generally have a higher resting metabolic rate, meaning they burn more calories even when inactive, which can aid in weight loss.

Metabolism and Genetics

Your metabolism is the process by which your body converts food and drink into energy. Understanding how metabolism truly works, and debunking common metabolism myths, can help you set realistic expectations for your weight loss journey, as metabolic rates vary widely among individuals due to genetics, age, sex, and muscle mass. A faster metabolism will naturally burn more calories, potentially leading to quicker weight loss with the same calorie intake compared to someone with a slower metabolism.

Calorie Deficit Consistency

The most crucial factor is consistently maintaining a calorie deficit—consuming fewer calories than your body burns. To lose one pound of fat, you generally need to create a deficit of approximately 3,500 calories. Therefore, to lose a stone (14 pounds), you need a total deficit of around 49,000 calories.

  • Moderate Deficit: A deficit of 500-1000 calories per day typically leads to 1-2 pounds of weight loss per week. This is widely considered a safe and sustainable approach.
  • Extreme Deficit: While larger deficits might lead to quicker initial weight loss, they are often unsustainable, can lead to nutrient deficiencies, muscle loss, and metabolic slowdown. As you progress, understanding conditions like metabolic adaptation can be crucial, as your body may adjust its energy expenditure over time, potentially impacting the rate and duration of weight loss. Such extreme deficits are generally not recommended without medical supervision.

Diet Quality and Macronutrient Balance

The types of food you eat also play a role. A diet rich in protein, fiber, and whole foods can help you feel fuller for longer, reduce cravings, and support muscle mass, all of which aid in weight loss. AI Weight Coach provides personalized meal plans to ensure you're getting balanced nutrition while staying within your calorie goals.

Physical Activity Level

Increasing your physical activity helps burn more calories, contributing to your overall calorie deficit. A combination of cardiovascular exercise (like walking, jogging, cycling) and strength training (to build muscle) is most effective for healthy, sustainable weight loss.

Sleep, Stress, and Hydration

  • Sleep: Lack of sleep can disrupt hormones that regulate appetite (ghrelin and leptin), leading to increased hunger and cravings.
  • Stress: Chronic stress elevates cortisol levels, which can promote fat storage, particularly around the abdomen.
  • Hydration: Drinking adequate water supports metabolism, can help you feel full, and prevents mistaking thirst for hunger.

So, How Long Does it Realistically Take to Lose a Stone?

Given the factors above, let's break down realistic timelines based on a healthy weight loss rate.

  1. If you lose 1 pound per week:

    14 pounds (1 stone) ÷ 1 pound/week = 14 weeks. This is approximately 3.5 months.

  2. If you lose 1.5 pounds per week:

    14 pounds (1 stone) ÷ 1.5 pounds/week = approximately 9.3 weeks. This is roughly 2.3 months.

  3. If you lose 2 pounds per week:

    14 pounds (1 stone) ÷ 2 pounds/week = 7 weeks. This is approximately 1.75 months.

These calculations illustrate that a stone can be lost in anywhere from 7 to 14 weeks when following a healthy and sustainable approach. It's important to remember that weight loss isn't always linear; you may experience plateaus or fluctuations, which are normal parts of the journey.

Strategies to Support Healthy Weight Loss

To achieve and maintain your weight loss goals, focus on integrating sustainable habits into your daily life:

1. Create a Moderate Calorie Deficit

Use a TDEE (Total Daily Energy Expenditure) calculator to estimate your daily calorie needs, then subtract 500-750 calories to create a deficit that supports 1-1.5 pounds of weight loss per week. Remember, these are estimates, and adjustments may be needed.

2. Prioritize Protein and Fiber

Foods rich in protein (lean meats, fish, eggs, legumes, tofu) and fiber (fruits, vegetables, whole grains) promote satiety, helping you feel full and satisfied, which reduces the likelihood of overeating.

3. Hydrate Regularly

Drink plenty of water throughout the day. Often, thirst is mistaken for hunger, leading to unnecessary snacking. Water also supports metabolic functions.

4. Incorporate Regular Physical Activity

Aim for a combination of:

  • Cardiovascular Exercise: 150-300 minutes of moderate-intensity activity per week (e.g., brisk walking, jogging, cycling).
  • Strength Training: Incorporating strength training workouts 2-3 sessions per week can significantly increase your muscle mass, which in turn boosts your resting metabolic rate and aids in consistent calorie burning.

5. Prioritize Sleep and Manage Stress

Aim for 7-9 hours of quality sleep per night. Practice stress-reducing techniques like meditation, yoga, or spending time in nature.

6. Track Your Progress

Monitoring your food intake and activity levels can provide valuable insights and keep you accountable. This doesn't necessarily mean daily weigh-ins, but consistent tracking of habits and occasional scale checks can be helpful.

Conclusion

Losing a stone is an achievable goal that, for most people, takes between 7 and 14 weeks with consistent effort and a focus on healthy habits. Celebrate small victories, learn from setbacks, and focus on overall health and well-being rather than just the number on the scale.
