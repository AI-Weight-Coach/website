Why Am I Not Losing Weight on Saxenda?

Jun 28, 2026 7 min read

If you've been prescribed a weight management medication and find that your weight loss progress isn't meeting expectations, it can be frustrating. While medications like Saxenda are effective tools, they work best when integrated into a comprehensive weight loss strategy. Not seeing the scale move could be attributed to various factors, including your dietary habits, physical activity levels, adherence to the medication regimen, individual physiological responses, or even underlying health conditions. It's crucial to approach this situation systematically and, most importantly, in consultation with your healthcare provider.

How Does This Type of Medication Assist with Weight Loss?

Medications like Saxenda are a type of GLP-1 receptor agonist, which means they mimic a natural hormone in your body called glucagon-like peptide-1. This hormone plays several roles in weight management:

  • Appetite Regulation: It helps you feel fuller sooner and for longer periods, reducing overall food intake.
  • Delayed Gastric Emptying: It slows down the movement of food from your stomach to your intestines, contributing to increased satiety.
  • Blood Sugar Control: While not its primary function for weight loss, it can also help regulate blood sugar levels, which can indirectly support weight management efforts.

The goal of such medication is to act as an adjunct to a reduced-calorie diet and increased physical activity, not as a standalone solution. It helps to manage hunger and cravings, making it easier for individuals to adhere to lifestyle changes.

What Are Common Reasons for a Weight Loss Plateau While Taking Medication?

Even with the support of medication, weight loss isn't always linear. Several factors can contribute to a stall or lack of progress:

1. Insufficient Calorie Deficit

Despite the medication helping to reduce appetite, it's still possible to consume more calories than your body needs to lose weight. True weight loss requires achieving and maintaining a consistent calorie deficit, which is fundamental as it forms the bedrock of any successful weight loss journey. Even small, unnoticed indulgences or portion creep can add up, neutralizing the appetite-suppressing effects of the medication.

2. Inadequate Lifestyle Changes

Medication is a tool, but it doesn't replace the fundamental need for lifestyle adjustments. If dietary habits haven't sufficiently changed (e.g., still consuming high-calorie, low-nutrient foods) or physical activity levels remain low, weight loss may be slow or nonexistent. It's important to adopt a balanced, nutrient-dense eating pattern and incorporate regular exercise. Integrating strength training workouts is crucial, as increased muscle mass boosts your resting metabolism, making it easier to burn calories and overcome plateaus.

3. Medication Adherence and Dosage

For the medication to be effective, it must be taken consistently and as prescribed by your doctor. Missing doses or not following the recommended titration schedule can impact its efficacy. Furthermore, your healthcare provider will guide you on the appropriate dosage, and it's essential to communicate any challenges you face in adhering to the regimen.

4. Individual Physiological Response

Everyone's body responds differently to medications. Some individuals may experience more significant appetite suppression, while others may not respond as robustly. Genetic factors, metabolism, and other biological elements can influence how effectively the medication works for you.

5. Underlying Health Conditions or Medications

Certain medical conditions, such as an underactive thyroid (hypothyroidism), polycystic ovary syndrome (PCOS), or sleep apnea, can make weight loss challenging. Additionally, some other medications (e.g., certain antidepressants, steroids) can promote weight gain or hinder weight loss efforts. Always ensure your healthcare provider is aware of your full medical history and all medications you are taking.

6. Stress, Sleep, and Hydration

These often-overlooked factors play a critical role in weight management. Chronic stress can elevate cortisol levels, which can promote fat storage, especially around the abdomen. Poor sleep disrupts hormones that regulate hunger and satiety, leading to increased cravings. Dehydration can sometimes be mistaken for hunger, leading to unnecessary calorie intake.

7. Weight Loss Plateaus

As you lose weight, your body's energy needs decrease. Understanding metabolic adaptation can shed light on why weight loss might slow down or stop, as your body adjusts its energy expenditure in response to prolonged calorie restriction. What once was a sufficient calorie deficit might no longer be. Plateaus are a normal part of the weight loss journey and often require adjustments to your diet and exercise plan. It's important to debunk common weight loss plateau myths to avoid frustration and ensure you're employing effective, evidence-based strategies to restart your progress.

When Should I Consult My Healthcare Provider About My Weight Loss Progress?

If you are not losing weight as expected while on a weight management medication, or if you're experiencing a prolonged plateau, it's always best to discuss this with your healthcare provider. They can:

  • Review Your Medication Adherence: Confirm you are taking the medication correctly and at the appropriate dosage.
  • Assess Lifestyle Factors: Discuss your current diet, exercise routine, sleep habits, and stress levels to identify areas for improvement.
  • Check for Underlying Issues: Rule out any medical conditions or other medications that might be hindering your progress.
  • Adjust Treatment Plan: They may recommend adjustments to your lifestyle strategy, consider alternative approaches, or explore other treatment options if appropriate.
  • Set Realistic Expectations: Ensure that your weight loss goals are healthy and achievable, especially in the context of your overall health.

Remember, weight management is a complex, personal journey. Medications like Saxenda are valuable tools, but they are most effective when part of a holistic plan that includes diet, exercise, and continuous medical guidance.
