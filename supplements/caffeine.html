Caffeine for Weight Loss

Updated Feb 23, 2026

Weak to Moderate
evidence
for significant weight loss
Evidence RatingWeak to Moderate
Recommended Dosage100-400 mg/day (not exceeding 400 mg daily for most healthy adults, ideally split into doses)
Mechanism of ActionCaffeine acts as a central nervous system stimulant, increasing thermogenesis (calorie burning) and fat oxidation. It can temporarily suppress appetite and enhance exercise performance, indirectly contributing to greater calorie expenditure.
Potential Side EffectsInsomnia, anxiety, jitters, increased heart rate, elevated blood pressure, and gastrointestinal upset. High doses can lead to headaches, tremors, and arrhythmias. Tolerance can develop, reducing its efficacy over time.
VerdictCaffeine provides a modest, temporary boost to metabolism and fat oxidation, but its standalone contribution to significant, sustained weight loss is limited and often diminished by tolerance. It is not a primary weight loss solution.

Does Caffeine Actually Help You Lose Weight?

Caffeine can acutely increase metabolic rate and fat oxidation, potentially aiding calorie expenditure. However, its overall impact on long-term, significant weight loss is modest and often subject to tolerance development, meaning effects may diminish over time. It's more of a minor adjunct rather than a primary driver for sustainable weight loss.
