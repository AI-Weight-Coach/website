Does Walking Help You Lose Weight Or Running?

Nov 27, 2026 6 min read

When it comes to shedding pounds, the age-old question often arises: does walking help you lose weight, or is running the superior choice? The truth is, both walking and running are highly effective forms of exercise for weight loss, primarily by contributing to a calorie deficit. While running typically burns more calories per minute due to its higher intensity, it also contributes to the EPOC or 'afterburn effect,' where your body continues to burn calories at an elevated rate for hours post-workout. The best activity for you ultimately depends on your fitness level, preferences, and most importantly, your ability to stick with it consistently.

How Does Exercise Lead to Weight Loss?

At its core, weight loss boils down to a simple principle: consuming fewer calories than you burn, which is known as creating a calorie deficit. Exercise, whether walking or running, significantly increases the number of calories your body expends, making it easier to achieve this deficit. Beyond the immediate calorie burn during the activity, exercise also contributes in other ways:

  • Increased Metabolism: Regular physical activity can boost your metabolic rate, meaning your body burns more calories even at rest.
  • EPOC (Excess Post-exercise Oxygen Consumption): Often called the "afterburn effect," higher intensity activities like running can cause your body to continue burning calories at an elevated rate for hours after your workout ends.
  • Muscle Preservation/Growth: Exercise, especially when combined with strength training, helps preserve and build muscle mass. Muscle is more metabolically active than fat, burning more calories at rest.

By understanding these mechanisms, you can appreciate that any movement contributing to a calorie deficit and improved metabolic health supports weight loss.

Walking vs. Running: The Calorie Burn Showdown

While both activities burn calories, there’s a difference in efficiency. Running, being a higher-impact and more vigorous activity, generally burns more calories in the same amount of time compared to walking. For example, a person weighing 150 pounds might burn approximately:

  • Walking at 3.5 mph: Around 270 calories per hour.
  • Running at 6 mph: Around 600 calories per hour.

These are approximate figures, and actual calorie burn varies based on factors like body weight, intensity, incline, and individual metabolism. However, this comparison clearly shows running's higher calorie expenditure per unit of time.

Walking: The Accessible & Sustainable Choice

Don't let running's higher calorie burn overshadow the immense benefits of walking. Walking, though less intense than running, is a highly effective and sustainable form of exercise for weight loss, especially for beginners or those seeking a lower-impact option.

  • Beginners: It requires no special skills or equipment (beyond comfortable shoes) and is easy to start.
  • Low Impact: It puts less stress on your joints, making it suitable for individuals with joint pain, those recovering from injuries, or those with higher body weights.
  • Accessibility: You can walk almost anywhere, anytime, making it easier to integrate into daily routines.
  • Sustainability: Because it's less taxing, walking is often easier to sustain for longer durations or more frequently throughout the week, leading to a significant cumulative calorie burn over time. A 90-minute walk could burn as many calories as a 45-minute run for some individuals.
  • Stress Reduction: Brisk walking can be a great way to de-stress, which can indirectly help with weight management by reducing emotional eating.

Even incorporating short, frequent walks can add up. Think about taking the stairs, parking further away, or walking during your lunch break.

Running: The High-Intensity Calorie Crusher

Running is an excellent choice for those seeking a higher-intensity workout and greater calorie expenditure in a shorter amount of time. Its benefits include:

  • Higher Calorie Burn: As mentioned, running burns more calories per minute, making it efficient for creating a calorie deficit.
  • Cardiovascular Health: It significantly improves heart and lung health, boosting endurance and stamina.
  • EPOC Effect: The higher intensity of running leads to a more pronounced afterburn effect, continuing to burn calories post-workout.
  • Mental Health Boost: Many runners report a strong sense of accomplishment and improved mood, often referred to as a "runner's high."

However, running is also higher impact and carries a greater risk of injury, especially for beginners or those with pre-existing joint conditions. Proper form, warm-ups, cool-downs, and gradual progression are crucial.

Which Activity is More Sustainable for You?

The most important factor for weight loss through exercise isn't just how many calories an activity burns per minute, but how consistently you can perform it over time. A workout you dread and skip regularly will yield fewer results than an activity you genuinely enjoy and do consistently.

Factors to Consider When Choosing Your Pace

  • Current Fitness Level: If you're new to exercise, walking is a safer and more manageable starting point. You can gradually increase speed, duration, or incline, eventually progressing to jogging or running if desired.
  • Joint Health: Individuals with knee, hip, or ankle issues might find walking more comfortable and less aggravating than running.
  • Time Availability: If you have limited time, running offers a more calorie-efficient workout. If you have more flexibility, longer walks can be equally effective.
  • Enjoyment: Do you find one more enjoyable than the other? Enjoyment is a powerful motivator for consistency.
  • Injury History: Be mindful of past injuries. High-impact activities like running can sometimes exacerbate old issues.

Beyond Calories: The Broader Benefits of Movement

Both walking and running offer a wealth of health benefits that extend beyond just burning calories and losing weight. These include:

  • Improved Mood and Mental Health: Exercise releases endorphins, reducing stress, anxiety, and symptoms of depression.
  • Better Sleep Quality: Regular physical activity can lead to deeper, more restorative sleep.
  • Reduced Risk of Chronic Diseases: Both activities contribute to lowering the risk of heart disease, type 2 diabetes, certain cancers, and high blood pressure.
  • Enhanced Bone Density: Weight-bearing exercises help strengthen bones.
  • Increased Energy Levels: Counterintuitively, exercise can actually boost your overall energy throughout the day.

How to Maximize Weight Loss with Walking or Running

No matter which you choose, here's how to get the most out of your efforts:

  1. Be Consistent: Aim for at least 150-300 minutes of moderate-intensity exercise (like brisk walking) or 75-150 minutes of vigorous-intensity exercise (like running) per week. Spreading it out across most days of the week is ideal.
  2. Progressive Overload: Gradually increase the duration, intensity (speed or incline), or frequency of your workouts. For walkers, try power walking, adding hills, or interval walking. For runners, try increasing distance, speed, or incorporating sprints.
  3. Combine with Strength Training: Incorporating 2-3 sessions of strength training per week will build muscle, further boost your metabolism, and improve overall body composition.
  4. Focus on Nutrition: Exercise is only one piece of the puzzle. To truly lose weight, you must also pay attention to your diet.   5. Listen to Your Body: Rest and recovery are just as important as the workouts themselves. Avoid overtraining to prevent injury and burnout.
  6. Stay Hydrated: Water is essential for all bodily functions, including metabolism and energy levels.

Ultimately, the choice between walking and running for weight loss isn't about one being inherently "better," but rather about which activity you can consistently integrate into your lifestyle. For many, a combination of both – perhaps walking on recovery days and running on others – offers the best of both worlds.
