Why Am I Not Losing Weight on Saxenda?

Jul 02, 2026 6 min read

While medications like Saxenda are powerful tools in weight management by helping reduce appetite and increase feelings of fullness, not losing weight can be frustrating and may indicate that other factors need attention. Effective weight loss, even with medication, still fundamentally requires a caloric deficit, and elements like diet, exercise, individual physiological responses, and lifestyle play critical roles. It's crucial to consult your healthcare provider to discuss your specific situation and explore potential adjustments to your weight loss strategy.

How Do GLP-1 Medications Like Saxenda Support Weight Loss?

Liraglutide, the active ingredient in Saxenda, belongs to a class of medications known as GLP-1 receptor agonists. These medications work by mimicking a natural hormone in your body that regulates appetite and food intake. Specifically, GLP-1:

  • Increases Satiety: It helps you feel fuller faster and for longer after eating, leading to reduced food intake.
  • Slows Gastric Emptying: Food stays in your stomach for a longer period, contributing to prolonged feelings of fullness.
  • Reduces Cravings: Many individuals report a decrease in food cravings, particularly for high-fat or sugary foods.

While these mechanisms significantly aid in creating a caloric deficit, they don't eliminate the need for conscious dietary choices and an active lifestyle. The medication is a valuable facilitator, but sustained weight loss often hinges on comprehensive lifestyle changes.

What Are Common Reasons for Not Losing Weight on a GLP-1 Medication?

If you're using Saxenda and not seeing the desired weight loss, it's important to consider a range of potential factors. Weight management is complex, and multiple elements can influence your progress.

Is Your Calorie Intake Still Too High?

Even with reduced appetite, it's possible to consume more calories than your body needs to lose weight. Foods high in unhealthy fats, refined sugars, or large portion sizes can quickly add up, overriding the medication's effects. Saxenda helps manage hunger, but it doesn't prevent you from overeating if you're not mindful of your food choices. It’s essential to focus on nutrient-dense, whole foods and understand Calorie Deficit Strategies to maintain an appropriate caloric deficit for your body.

  • Mindful Eating: Pay attention to hunger and fullness cues, eating slowly and savoring your meals.
  • Portion Control: Even healthy foods need to be consumed in appropriate amounts.
  • Food Choices: Prioritize lean protein, fiber-rich vegetables, fruits, and whole grains. Exploring structured options like High-Protein Low-Calorie Meal Plans can further support your goals.

Are You Getting Enough Physical Activity?

Exercise is a crucial component of any successful weight loss strategy. While medication can help with appetite, physical activity burns calories, builds muscle mass (which boosts metabolism), and improves overall health. If your physical activity level is low, you might not be burning enough calories to achieve the necessary deficit for weight loss.

  • Aerobic Exercise: Aim for at least 150 minutes of moderate-intensity activity per week, incorporating Effective Cardio for Overall Fat Loss (e.g., brisk walking, swimming, cycling).
  • Strength Training: Incorporate muscle-building exercises 2-3 times per week to preserve and increase lean muscle mass.
  • Non-Exercise Activity Thermogenesis (NEAT): Increase daily movement, like taking the stairs or parking further away.

Is Your Dosage Optimal and Your Body Responding As Expected?

Individuals respond differently to medications. While many people experience significant appetite suppression and weight loss, some may have a less pronounced response. It's possible that your body requires a different approach, or you may not yet be at the therapeutic dose that offers optimal effects. Dosage adjustments should only be made by your prescribing healthcare provider.

Could Underlying Medical Conditions Be Playing a Role?

Certain medical conditions can make weight loss more challenging, even with medication. These include:

  • Thyroid Disorders: An underactive thyroid (hypothyroidism) can slow metabolism.
  • Polycystic Ovary Syndrome (PCOS): Can affect hormones and insulin resistance, making weight loss difficult.
  • Other Medications: Some medications for conditions like depression, anxiety, or certain chronic illnesses can lead to weight gain or hinder weight loss.

Discuss any known or suspected medical conditions and all other medications you are taking with your doctor.

How Are Your Lifestyle Factors Like Sleep and Stress?

Poor sleep and chronic stress can significantly impact your weight loss efforts. Lack of sleep disrupts hunger-regulating hormones (ghrelin and leptin), leading to increased appetite and cravings. Stress elevates cortisol levels, which can promote fat storage, especially around the abdomen, and may lead to emotional eating.

  • Prioritize Sleep: Aim for 7-9 hours of quality sleep per night.
  • Manage Stress: Practice relaxation techniques such as meditation, deep breathing, yoga, or spending time in nature.

Are You Experiencing a Weight Loss Plateau?

It's common to experience plateaus during a weight loss journey, and understanding common weight loss plateau myths can help you navigate this challenge. As you lose weight, your body's metabolic rate can decrease because you weigh less, requiring fewer calories to maintain your new weight. What worked initially might need adjustment. Your body is incredibly adaptive, and sometimes a change in strategy is necessary to kickstart further progress.

Are You Taking the Medication Correctly and Consistently?

Proper adherence to your medication regimen is vital for its effectiveness. Ensure you are taking Saxenda exactly as prescribed by your healthcare provider, including the correct dose and administration technique. Missing doses or not following instructions can reduce its impact.

What Steps Can You Take If Weight Loss Stalls on Saxenda?

If you're not losing weight on Saxenda, don't get discouraged. There are actionable steps you can take:

  1. Consult Your Healthcare Provider: This is the most important first step. Your doctor can assess your dosage, rule out underlying medical conditions, review your overall health, and offer personalized advice. They might adjust your treatment plan or recommend further investigations.
  2. Re-evaluate Your Dietary Habits: Even with reduced hunger, scrutinize your food choices. Are you consuming hidden calories from drinks, sauces, or snacks? Focus on whole, unprocessed foods. Tools like AI Weight Coach can help you accurately track your caloric and macronutrient intake, providing clarity on where adjustments might be needed.
  3. Increase and Diversify Physical Activity: If you're not active, start slowly and gradually increase your exercise. If you're already active, consider intensifying your workouts, trying new activities, or incorporating more strength training to boost your metabolism.
  4. Optimize Sleep and Stress Management: Implement strategies to improve sleep hygiene and reduce stress levels. These lifestyle changes can have a profound impact on your body's ability to lose weight.
  5. Practice Patience and Consistency: Weight loss is rarely a linear process. Be patient with yourself and consistent with your efforts. Small, sustainable changes over time yield the best results.
  6. Leverage Support Tools: Utilize apps like AI Weight Coach to help you set realistic goals, track progress, and make informed decisions about your nutrition and activity. Consistent data can reveal patterns you might otherwise miss.
