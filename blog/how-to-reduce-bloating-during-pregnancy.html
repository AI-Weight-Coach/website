How to Reduce Bloating During Pregnancy

Nov 27, 2026 8 min read

Bloating is a common and often uncomfortable symptom many women experience during pregnancy. Bloating is often a key symptom of indigestion in pregnancy, stemming from hormonal shifts, slower digestive processes, and the pressure of the expanding uterus. While it's a normal part of the physiological changes your body undergoes, there are several practical strategies you can adopt to help reduce its intensity and find relief. To lessen bloating, focus on eating smaller, more frequent meals, staying consistently hydrated, identifying and limiting gas-producing foods, and incorporating gentle, regular physical activity into your routine. Always consult your healthcare provider for personalized advice and to ensure any changes are safe for your specific pregnancy.

Why Does Bloating Happen During Pregnancy?

Understanding the root causes of pregnancy bloating can help you address it more effectively. Several factors contribute to this common discomfort:

Hormonal Changes

One of the primary culprits is the surge in progesterone levels during pregnancy. Progesterone is essential for maintaining the pregnancy, but it also relaxes smooth muscles throughout your body, including those in your digestive tract. This relaxation slows down digestion, allowing more time for gas to accumulate, which can lead to bloating, gas, and constipation.

Slowed Digestion

As progesterone slows down your digestive system, food moves through your intestines more sluggishly. This prolonged transit time can lead to increased fermentation by gut bacteria, producing more gas. The slower digestion also means more water is absorbed from the stool, potentially leading to constipation, which further exacerbates bloating.

Uterine Pressure

As your uterus expands to accommodate your growing baby, it puts increasing pressure on your stomach and intestines. This physical pressure can hinder normal digestion and contribute to feelings of fullness and bloating, especially in the later trimesters.

What Dietary Changes Can Help Alleviate Pregnancy Bloating?

Your diet plays a significant role in managing pregnancy bloating. Implementing an anti-bloating diet can significantly reduce discomfort by focusing on easy-to-digest foods and avoiding gas-producing culprits that often worsen pregnancy bloating. Small, mindful adjustments to your eating habits can make a big difference.

Embrace Fiber-Rich Foods (Gradually)

While too much fiber too quickly can cause gas, a gradual increase in soluble and insoluble fiber can help prevent constipation, a major contributor to bloating. Opt for whole grains, fruits like berries and apples (with skin), vegetables, and legumes. Introduce them slowly to allow your digestive system to adapt.

Limit Gas-Producing Foods

Certain foods are notorious for producing gas in some individuals. While not everyone reacts the same way, consider reducing or temporarily avoiding:

  • Cruciferous vegetables: Broccoli, cabbage, cauliflower, Brussels sprouts. Try steaming them thoroughly, which can make them easier to digest.
  • Legumes: Beans, lentils. Soaking and rinsing them thoroughly before cooking can help.
  • Artificial sweeteners: Sorbitol, xylitol, mannitol, often found in sugar-free products.
  • Fried and fatty foods: These take longer to digest.
  • High-sugar foods: Especially those with refined sugars.

Keeping a food diary can help you identify your personal triggers. AI Weight Coach, for example, can be a helpful tool for logging your food intake and noting how you feel afterwards, allowing you to spot patterns between what you eat and your bloating symptoms.

Smaller, More Frequent Meals

Instead of three large meals, try eating five or six smaller meals throughout the day. This reduces the burden on your digestive system, preventing it from becoming overwhelmed and minimizing the amount of gas produced at any one time.

Avoid Carbonated Drinks

The bubbles in carbonated beverages introduce extra air into your digestive tract, directly contributing to bloating. Opt for plain water, herbal teas (check with your doctor for pregnancy-safe options), or diluted fruit juices instead.

Mindful Eating Practices

Eating too quickly or talking while you eat can lead to swallowing excess air. Practice mindful eating by chewing your food thoroughly, eating slowly, and taking smaller bites. This also aids digestion by giving your body time to process the food efficiently.

How Can Hydration and Lifestyle Choices Reduce Bloating?

Beyond diet, several lifestyle adjustments can significantly impact your comfort levels.

Drink Plenty of Water

It might seem counterintuitive to drink more water when you feel bloated, but adequate hydration is crucial. Water helps soften stool, preventing constipation, and aids in the efficient movement of food through your digestive system. Aim for at least 8-10 glasses of water daily, sipping it throughout the day rather than chugging large amounts at once.

Engage in Gentle Exercise

Engaging in pregnancy-safe exercises, such as walking or prenatal yoga, can stimulate digestion and help alleviate gas and bloating, contributing to overall well-being during pregnancy. Regular physical activity, even light exercise, can also stimulate bowel movements and help release trapped gas. Always consult your doctor before starting any new exercise regimen during pregnancy.

Wear Comfortable Clothing

Tight clothing around your waist can put additional pressure on your abdomen, exacerbating discomfort. Opt for loose-fitting, comfortable maternity clothes that don't restrict your stomach.

Manage Stress

Stress can impact digestion and lead to symptoms like bloating. Incorporate stress-reducing activities into your daily routine, such as meditation, deep breathing exercises, gentle walks, or simply taking time for yourself. Pregnancy can be a stressful time, and prioritizing mental well-being benefits your physical health too.

Ensure Adequate Rest

Fatigue can worsen digestive issues. Ensure you're getting enough sleep and taking breaks when needed. Rest allows your body to function optimally, including your digestive system.

When Should You Talk to Your Doctor About Pregnancy Bloating?

While bloating is generally normal during pregnancy, there are times when it warrants a conversation with your healthcare provider:

  • Severe or persistent pain: If your bloating is accompanied by intense abdominal pain that doesn't subside.
  • Sudden onset: If bloating appears very suddenly and severely, especially if accompanied by other concerning symptoms.
  • Accompanied by other symptoms: Such as fever, chills, nausea, vomiting, severe diarrhea, or blood in your stool.
  • Significant weight loss or inability to keep food down: Though not typical for bloating, these are red flags for other issues.
  • Signs of preeclampsia: In later pregnancy, sudden swelling (edema) in your face, hands, or feet, along with severe headaches or vision changes, requires immediate medical attention. While not directly related to gas bloating, it's important to differentiate.

Your doctor can rule out any underlying conditions and offer tailored advice, ensuring your health and your baby's well-being.

Finding Relief and Comfort

Pregnancy is a remarkable journey, but it comes with its share of discomforts, including bloating. By implementing these practical dietary and lifestyle changes, you can often find significant relief. Remember that every pregnancy is unique, and what works for one person might not work for another. Be patient with your body, listen to its signals, and don't hesitate to seek guidance from your healthcare provider.
