How to Reduce Bloating in 24 Hours

Oct 24, 2026 6 min read

Feeling uncomfortably full or swollen can disrupt your day, but the good news is that many instances of bloating can be significantly reduced within a single day. By making strategic adjustments to your diet – focusing on foods that minimize gas production and support healthy digestion, a core principle of anti-bloating diets – along with mindful hydration and daily habits, you can often find relief from that uncomfortable distension quickly. This guide will walk you through actionable steps to help you feel lighter and more comfortable within 24 hours.

What Causes Bloating and Why Does it Happen?

Bloating is a common digestive issue characterized by a feeling of fullness, tightness, or swelling in the abdomen. While often benign, it can be quite uncomfortable. The primary culprits behind bloating typically include:

  • Gas Production: This is perhaps the most common cause. Certain foods produce more gas during digestion, especially those high in fermentable carbohydrates (FODMAPs). Swallowing air while eating or drinking quickly can also contribute.
  • Constipation: Irregular bowel movements can lead to a buildup of stool and gas in the intestines, causing distension.
  • Water Retention: High sodium intake, hormonal fluctuations, or dehydration can cause the body to hold onto excess fluid, leading to a puffy feeling.
  • Food Sensitivities: Conditions like lactose intolerance or gluten sensitivity can trigger bloating and other digestive symptoms after consuming trigger foods.
  • Dietary Choices: Eating too quickly, consuming large meals, or opting for carbonated drinks, artificial sweeteners, and certain fibrous vegetables can all contribute to bloating.

Immediate Strategies: How to Reduce Bloating Today

When you need quick relief, focusing on a few key areas can make a significant difference. Here’s how you can actively work to reduce bloating within 24 hours:

Hydrate Smartly: Is More Water the Answer to Bloating?

It might seem counterintuitive, but drinking more water can actually help reduce bloating. Dehydration can signal your body to retain water, leading to puffiness. Adequate hydration helps to:

  • Aid Digestion: Water helps break down food and move it through your digestive tract, preventing constipation.
  • Flush Excess Sodium: If your bloating is due to high salt intake, water helps your kidneys excrete excess sodium.

Aim to sip water throughout the day. Consider also incorporating:

  • Herbal Teas for Digestion: While carbonated drinks can contribute to bloating, opting for specific beverages like herbal teas can soothe the digestive system and aid in reducing discomfort. Peppermint, ginger, and chamomile teas are known for their soothing effects on the digestive system and can help relax intestinal muscles, reducing gas and cramping.
  • Lemon Water: A squeeze of lemon can act as a mild diuretic and aid digestion.

Mind Your Meals: What to Eat (and Avoid) for Quick Relief

Your dietary choices are crucial for rapid bloating reduction. Focus on easily digestible foods and avoid common triggers.

Eat Smaller, More Frequent Meals

Overloading your digestive system with large meals can exacerbate bloating. Opt for smaller, more frequent meals throughout the day. This keeps your digestive system working steadily without being overwhelmed.

Chew Thoroughly and Eat Slowly

Digestion begins in the mouth. Chewing your food thoroughly helps break it down mechanically, making it easier for your stomach and intestines to process. Eating slowly also reduces the amount of air you swallow, a common cause of gas and bloating. Put your fork down between bites and savor your food.

Identify and Avoid Trigger Foods

Certain foods are notorious for causing bloating. For quick relief, it's best to avoid them for 24 hours:

  • Carbonated Drinks: The bubbles introduce air into your digestive system.
  • Artificial Sweeteners: Sorbitol, xylitol, and other sugar alcohols are poorly absorbed and can cause gas and bloating.
  • High-Sodium Foods: Processed foods, canned soups, and fast food often contain high levels of sodium, leading to water retention.
  • Cruciferous Vegetables (Raw): While healthy, raw broccoli, cabbage, and cauliflower can be harder to digest and produce more gas. Cooked versions might be tolerated better.
  • Legumes: Beans and lentils are rich in fiber and oligosaccharides, which can cause gas.
  • Dairy (if lactose intolerant): If you suspect lactose intolerance, avoid dairy products.

Focus on Gentle, Digestible Foods

For the next 24 hours, choose foods that are known to be gentle on the stomach:

  • Bananas: High in potassium, which can help counteract sodium and reduce water retention.
  • Melon: Water-rich and easy to digest.
  • Cooked Vegetables: Spinach, zucchini, cucumbers (peeled), and carrots are less likely to cause gas than raw or cruciferous options.
  • Lean Protein: Grilled chicken, fish, or tofu can be good options.
  • Brown Rice or Quinoa: Easily digestible grains in moderate portions.
  • Fennel: Has antispasmodic properties that can help relax digestive muscles and reduce gas.

Get Moving: Does Light Exercise Help with Bloating?

Yes, incorporating gentle exercises for bloating relief can be incredibly effective in reducing bloating quickly. Beyond dietary changes, incorporating gentle movement into your day can stimulate bowel function and help expel trapped gas, offering quick relief from abdominal distension. Movement helps to:

  • Release Trapped Gas: Gentle exercise stimulates the movement of gas through your digestive tract, helping it to escape.
  • Improve Bowel Motility: Physical activity encourages regular bowel movements, preventing constipation-related bloating.

Even a 20-30 minute walk can make a noticeable difference. Gentle yoga poses, especially twists and inversions, can also be beneficial.

Manage Stress: How Does Stress Affect Digestion?

The gut-brain axis is powerful. Stress and anxiety can significantly impact your digestive system, leading to symptoms like bloating, gas, and constipation. When stressed, your body diverts resources away from digestion, slowing things down. To combat this:

  • Deep Breathing Exercises: Taking a few minutes for deep, diaphragmatic breathing can activate your parasympathetic nervous system, promoting relaxation and aiding digestion.
  • Mindfulness: Even short periods of meditation or simply focusing on the present moment can reduce stress levels.
  • Relaxation Techniques: A warm bath, listening to calming music, or light stretching can help ease tension.

Consider Gut Support (with caution)

While not an immediate fix, certain natural aids can offer gentle support:

  • Ginger: Known for its anti-inflammatory and digestive properties, ginger can help relieve nausea and gas. Try ginger tea or add fresh ginger to your meals.
  • Peppermint Oil: Some studies suggest peppermint oil can help relax the smooth muscles of the intestines, reducing spasms and gas. (Always consult a healthcare provider before taking supplements.)
  • Probiotic-Rich Foods: While full benefits take time, fermented foods like yogurt (if dairy-tolerant), kefir, sauerkraut, or kimchi can introduce beneficial bacteria to your gut, potentially improving digestion over time.
